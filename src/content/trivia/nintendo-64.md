Both of this console's best-remembered games are in The Strong National Museum
of Play's World Video Game Hall of Fame, and the museum's records single out
different things about them.

GoldenEye 007 was inducted with the class of 2025. The record dates the start of
development to 1994, when Rare and Nintendo began work on a game drawn from the
James Bond film released the following year. What the museum credits for the
game's standing is not the single-player campaign but the split-screen mode for
up to four players, and its verdict is that the game showed a first-person
shooter could work on a console rather than only on a personal computer. It puts
sales above eight million and places the game third on the console behind Super
Mario 64 and Mario Kart 64.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time followed with the class of 2022. The record
describes a game built around moving between two time periods, with Link's
actions as a child changing the world he returns to as an adult. It singles out
the lock-on targeting system, which it credits with avoiding the camera problems
of earlier 3D adventures, and says other developers took the idea up. United
States preorders passed 500,000 before release, and the museum puts lifetime
sales above 7.6 million.
