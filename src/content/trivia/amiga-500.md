---
title: Sold where the 64 was sold
sourceIds:
  - computing-history-amiga-a500
---

The Centre for Computing History's collection record describes the A500 as a
return to Commodore's roots. Where the Amiga 1000 went through computer stores,
the A500 was sold in the same mass retail outlets as the Commodore 64. The
record calls it Commodore's best-selling Amiga model and says it did
particularly well in Europe.

It also records how that was achieved in one market. In October 1989 the British
price fell from £499 to £399 and the machine was bundled with the Batman Pack,
and the record credits that drop with helping Commodore sell more than a million
A500s during the year.

The same record dates the announcement to the winter Consumer Electronics Show
of January 1987, alongside the high-end Amiga 2000. On what people did with the
machine the record hedges, and the hedge is kept here: hobbyists liked it, but
arguably its most widespread use was as a games machine.
