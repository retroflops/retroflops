Two Game Boy games are in The Strong National Museum of Play's World Video Game
Hall of Fame, and the museum's records credit both of them to the hardware
around the screen rather than to the screen.

Tetris went in with the class of 2015. The record traces it to Alexey Pajitnov,
a programmer in the Soviet Union who adapted a pentomino puzzle for computers in
1984 and named the result after the Greek word for four and after tennis. It
reached personal computers in North America and Europe in 1987. What the museum
says made it universal is that Nintendo packaged it with the Game Boy at the
handheld's 1989 debut.

Pokémon Red and Green followed in 2017. The record says that Satoshi Tajiri, who
had collected insects as a child, looked at the Game Boy's Game Link Cable and
pictured an insect crawling along it from one system to the other. Nintendo
published the pair in Japan in 1996 with 151 creatures to collect, and some of
them could be had only by trading with another player over that cable.
