The Science Museum Group holds a 1986 Model B, catalogued as object 2022-651.
Its curatorial note explains what the machine was made for. Acorn designed and
built it between 1981 and 1994 for the government-backed BBC Computer Literacy
Project, which set out to prepare people for what microprocessors and home
computers were going to do, using television and radio programs, publications,
software and courses alongside the hardware.

The note gives one number for how far that went: around 80 percent of British
schools had one. The museum's reading is that this introduced a generation of
schoolchildren both to computers and to programming as something creative rather
than merely technical. It traces the line forward too, to ARM, whose processors
now run in devices from phones to the supercomputer Fugaku.

The same collection holds a stranger object, 2008-5: a gold-plated BBC Micro
with two one-megabyte disc drives, made in 1985. It was a competition prize for
the second anniversary of The Micro User magazine, and entrants had to describe
five articles they expected the magazine to print within three years. The
curatorial note follows the plating through its trade: a gold casing built by
Universal Communications Company over more than a month, base units worked by
hand so the gold would take, and the dipping done by B & W Plating in Crawley.
