---
title: The machine everyone else copied
sourceIds:
  - chm-timeline-1981
---

The Computer History Museum's timeline entry for 1981 gives this machine its
formal name, the IBM Model 5150, and its parts: a 4.77 MHz Intel 8088 and
Microsoft's MS-DOS. The museum's reading is that IBM's brand recognition and a
large marketing campaign are what set the personal computer market growing
quickly, and that the machine changed business computing by being taken up
across industry.

The more consequential part of the entry is about what other companies did next.
The museum records the machine being widely cloned, and an ecosystem of
software, peripherals and commodity parts growing up around the platform as a
result.

A companion entry on the same page follows the software half. MS-DOS shipped
with the machine and began a long partnership between IBM and Microsoft, a
company Bill Gates and Paul Allen had founded six years earlier. The museum
notes that the imitators copied IBM's hardware, but mostly licensed Microsoft's
operating system rather than writing their own.
