title: The program that made it a business machine

The Computer History Museum's Revolution exhibition puts the Apple II's
commercial success down to two later additions rather than to the machine as it
shipped in 1977. The floppy disk drive of 1978 and the spreadsheet program
VisiCalc of 1979 are what the exhibition text says made it a blockbuster.

The museum's timeline entry for 1979 names Dan Bricklin, then an MBA candidate
at Harvard, and the programmer Bob Frankston as VisiCalc's authors, and states
that it was developed for the Apple II and boosted the machine's sales. What the
program did was recalculate a sheet of figures automatically, which the museum
describes as letting someone ask "What if?" questions of their financial
information.

The exhibition is careful about what the machine was sold as. It describes the
Apple II as promoted for ordinary people and marketed as an everyday tool for
home, work, or school, and it contrasts that with the Apple I, which left the
buyer to supply a case and a power supply.
