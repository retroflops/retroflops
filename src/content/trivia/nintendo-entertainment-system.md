Two of this console's defining games are in The Strong National Museum of Play's
World Video Game Hall of Fame, and the museum's records describe two different
kinds of influence.

Super Mario Bros. went in with the inaugural class of 2015. The record credits
Shigeru Miyamoto as its designer and Nintendo as its publisher, gives 1985 as
the year it appeared on the Famicom and on its American counterpart, and makes a
point about timing. The American home video game market had already crashed, and
the museum's judgment is that Mario helped Nintendo revive it.

The Legend of Zelda followed in 2016. The museum's record describes a game with
no set path, where players were free to work through Hyrule in whatever order
they chose, and says the resulting complexity pushed players into trading tips
with one another. Nintendo answered that with the Fun Club newsletter, which
became Nintendo Power. The record also notes a piece of hardware inside the
cartridge: an internal battery that backed up saved data.
