# A decade of Commodore clocks

Three machines from one company, all in their PAL configuration, because a PAL
Commodore and an NTSC Commodore use different clocks and mixing them would put
an artifact of broadcast timing into the comparison.

The clock row works: all three figures come from each machine's own hardware
documentation, they describe the main processor, and they were obtained the same
way, so the ratios are legitimate. They are also close to meaningless as a
statement about performance. A 68EC020 at 14.19 MHz does far more per cycle than
a 6510 at 0.98525 MHz, and nothing in a clock frequency knows that. The
multiplier here is a fact about oscillators.

Each machine carries more than one thing that answers to "memory": the Commodore
64 has RAM and ROM, and the Amiga 1200 has Chip RAM and a Kickstart ROM. All are
recorded as system memory. Asking for "the memory capacity" of such a machine is
asking two questions at once, so there is no single figure to divide. The
individual capacities are all there to read, but no multiplier is calculated.
