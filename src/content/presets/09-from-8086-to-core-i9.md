# From 8086 to Core i9

These representative PCs span forty-five years, from a 5 MHz 8086 system in 1978
to a Core i9-14900K system in 2023. The nominal-clock and whole-system
memory-capacity rows put the recorded figures next to one another, including the
unresolved E6600 clock and fitted RAM. The gaps are part of the comparison: an
Intel family row that combines two models cannot establish a value for one of
them.

The provisional Dhrystone MIPS-per-megahertz figures cannot support a speed
headline. They all name Dhrystone 2.1 and share a comparability group. A
benchmark result normalized by clock remains one benchmark result, not a general
claim about computer speed. The comparison shows the figures without automatic
multipliers.

The 14900K also makes "the clock" less singular than it was in 1978: its P-core
base frequency, E-core base frequency, and turbo maximum describe different
operating conditions. The preset keeps base and peak clocks in their separate
method rows. The provisional evidence and unresolved middle configuration keep
this page descriptive rather than a ranking.
